# Identifier accuracy (Milestone 5)

Generated by `test/core/audio/chord_accuracy_test.dart` (run `flutter test
test/core/audio/chord_accuracy_test.dart` to reprint). These are
**synthesised** strums (Karplus–Strong, the library's first shape for each
chord). A real guitar through a phone mic will score lower; recordings dropped
into `test/fixtures/wav/` as `chord_<Symbol>_<anything>.wav` (48 kHz) are
scored by the same test.

## How it works

1. **Chroma.** A 16,384-point FFT (about 3 Hz bins at 48 kHz, enough to tell
   low E from F) is reduced to spectral peaks. Each peak is mapped to its
   pitch class; peaks more than about 35 cents from a semitone are ignored,
   and high partials are down-weighted because they're mostly harmonics, not
   notes.
2. **Templates.** Each candidate chord is modelled as its tones plus their
   first six harmonics, which is what a string actually puts into the chroma.
   Cosine similarity ranks them. The candidates are the v1 qualities: major,
   minor, 7, maj7, m7, sus2, sus4 and 5, on all 12 roots. A small prior
   prefers the simpler name in a near-tie.
3. **Bass.** The lowest reasonably strong partial below 260 Hz. Having the
   root in the bass helps a candidate; a bass outside the chord counts
   against it.
4. **Single note or chord.** Note names can't separate them, because one D
   string's harmonics *are* D, F♯ and A. Frequency ratios can: a single
   string's partials sit at whole multiples of its pitch, while a chord has
   partials at ratios like 1.5. The identifier says "That's one note: E"
   instead of naming a chord.
5. **Smoothing.** Scores are averaged within a strum and reset at each new
   strum. The label changes only after the new chord has led for 2 frames
   (about 0.17 s). Once a strum has settled, the same chord gaining or losing
   notes (D → D5 as the high E fades; C → Cmaj7 from harmonics) keeps its
   name.
6. **Honesty.** Confidence compares the leader with its best real rival (not
   C vs Cmaj7). Below 0.35 the screen says **Not sure**. Nothing is named
   until a **strum has been heard** (a sudden jump in level), so steady
   background sound like a fan, a TV or the room can't produce a chord
   however chord-like it is. This rule came from the Android emulator: its
   microphone heard the room and claimed "B7, fairly sure".

**Known weakness:** 7th chords whose 7th sits on one string, or where the
triad's harmonics also land on the 7th (G7, Cmaj7, Fmaj7), are often named
as the plain triad. That's the safer error (the triad is still right) but
an error. Two-string power chords (root and fifth only) sometimes read as
sus2, because the fifth's own 3rd harmonic is the 2nd.

## Other checks

- **Single strings:** every open string is reported as a single note in more
  than 80% of frames, never as a confident chord.
- **Silence:** shows nothing.
- **Steady tonal background** (four sine tones at about −38 dBFS) gets no
  chord name.
- **Broadband noise** is never a confident chord.
- **Chord change** (C then G): the label goes C → G with nothing in between,
  within 0.7 s of the G strum.

## Not measured yet

Real guitars, real phone mics, real rooms. The single biggest unknown is how
much a phone's microphone colours the low strings. Recordings in
`test/fixtures/wav/` will answer it.

## Real-world conditions (2026-11-07)

A user reported Em reading as E5 and Am as A5 with wired EarPods held
near the guitar. Reproduced with a slow strum through a bassy headset-mic
model: the smoother's family lock (meant to stop a fading D becoming D5)
kept the power chord it settled on first, before the third arrived.

Every chord the lessons teach, plus common extras, was then run under ten
conditions (`test/core/audio/chord_conditions_test.dart`): strum speed,
up-strums, a close mic hearing the bass strings 9 dB louder (realistic)
and 18 dB louder (extreme), uneven picking, and a distant detuned phone in
room noise. Before the fixes, 38 of 296 chord/condition pairs read the
wrong name; after them, the only misses are Gmaj7 (not taught; its
library shape has the 7th only on the high E string) and G7/Dsus2 under
the extreme mic, where they read as G/D5, which practice accepts as
"close". Fixes:

- the family lock lets a power chord become its triad (E5 → Em), never
  the reverse;
- chroma uses magnitude^0.35 (was ^0.5), so string-level imbalance from
  mic placement counts for less;
- power and sus chords score lower with a non-root bass (D5 over a low A
  is Asus4; A5 over a low E is Esus4);
- the B minor lesson shows the A-shape (x24432) it describes.
