Steel-string acoustics: E, Em and other open chords now register in practice.

> **Pre-release.** Installs over 0.10.0 and keeps your progress, songs, chords and settings. Listening is measured on synthesised guitars through modelled mics; it has not been measured with a real guitar yet.

## Fixed

- **E and Em (and A, G, C, Am) often didn't count on a bright steel-string guitar.** A steel string's low E rings loud overtones: its 5th harmonic is a G♯, so an Em's three E strings put almost as much G♯ in the sound as the G you're fretting, and the B string's overtones add F♯ and D♯. Practice took those overtones for wrong notes and rejected the chord.
- **Practice now tells notes you play from overtones.** A pitch at a whole multiple of a lower ringing string, with that string's other overtones present, is treated as an overtone. In Em the G♯ sits at 5× the low E, so it's an overtone; in E the G♯ you fret has no lower note it could come from, so it counts.
- **A wrong third no longer slips through when the identifier names the chord outright.** On a bright guitar an Em could be named E, and a Dm named D. The major/minor check now applies to every strum, on every Chord matching setting.

## Measured

On a modelled bright steel-string acoustic, strummed 45 ways (near and far from the bridge, new to duller strings, phone mics losing more or less bass), across E, Em, A, Am, D, Dm, G and C:

| | 0.10.0 | 0.10.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Right chord counted | 292 of 360 | 358 of 360 |
| Wrong third counted (Em for E, Dm for D…) | 11 of 270 | 0 of 270 |

The earlier five-guitar matrix (steel, bright, nylon, old strings, thin; headset, far mic, uneven strums) is unchanged. The Identify screen and rhythm lessons are unchanged.
